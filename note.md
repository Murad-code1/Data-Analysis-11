## Methodology

Loaded the grocery transaction dataset using pandas  
Inspected dataset structure, validated data types, and checked for missing values  
Converted the Date column into datetime format for accurate time-based analysis  
Created basket-level transactions using Member_number and Date combinations  
Calculated basket size metrics to analyze the number of products purchased per transaction  
Performed customer purchase frequency analysis using unique shopping days per customer  
Segmented customers into multiple shopping frequency groups for behavioral analysis  
Analyzed product popularity by calculating total purchase counts for each grocery item  
Extracted weekday information from transaction dates to identify shopping behavior trends across the week  
Generated product co-occurrence combinations to identify products frequently purchased together  
Built professional visualizations using matplotlib and seaborn to explore customer behavior, basket distribution, and product demand patterns  

## Business Insights

Strong Demand for Essential Grocery Products: Products such as whole milk, other vegetables, and rolls/buns dominate customer purchases across the dataset. → Grocery stores should prioritize inventory availability for high-demand staple products to avoid stock shortages and maintain customer satisfaction.

Most Customers Shop Infrequently: Approximately 68% of customers shop less than once per month, indicating that many customers make occasional grocery visits rather than highly frequent purchases. → Retailers should implement loyalty programs, discount campaigns, and personalized promotions to encourage repeat shopping behavior.

Basket Sizes Remain Relatively Small: The average basket size is approximately 3.95 products, while single-item baskets account for nearly 17% of all transactions. → Stores can improve basket expansion opportunities through cross-selling strategies, bundle promotions, and better in-store product placement.

Thursday Shows the Highest Shopping Activity: Transaction analysis reveals that Thursday is the busiest shopping day in the dataset. → Grocery stores should allocate additional staff, optimize checkout operations, and increase inventory replenishment before high-traffic days.

Frequently Purchased Product Pairs Reveal Cross-Selling Opportunities: Product co-occurrence analysis shows that several grocery items are repeatedly purchased together within the same basket. → Retailers can place complementary products near each other to improve customer convenience and increase impulse purchases.

Customer Activity is Broadly Distributed: The top 10 customers contribute only a small portion of total shopping activity, indicating that sales are distributed across a wide customer base. → Business growth strategies should focus on improving engagement across all customer groups instead of depending heavily on a few high-frequency shoppers.

Weekday Shopping Patterns Affect Operational Planning: Shopping behavior changes across different weekdays, with variations in both transaction volume and average basket size. → Stores should align staffing schedules, shelf replenishment timing, and operational planning with weekday demand patterns.

Medium-Sized Baskets Dominate Grocery Purchases: Most transactions contain a moderate number of products, reflecting routine household shopping behavior. → Promotional campaigns targeting commonly purchased household products may improve customer retention and overall sales performance.
